# Challenge brief

**Identify Earth Locations that Analog the Permanent Moon Base Locations and Mars**
NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 · Difficulty: Advanced / Intermediate / Beginner · Subjects: Earth Science, Planets & Moons, Software, Space Exploration
Official page: https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/permanent-moon-base-locations-and-mars/

## The ask

Use open Earth, Moon and Mars data to search Earth for places, environments and processes that could serve as analogs for **candidate permanent Moon-base locations** or **future Mars landing and exploration sites**. Identify and characterize one or more Earth locations, explain:

- which characteristics or processes make a strong analogy,
- where the analogy breaks down,
- how studying or using the analog would help prepare for Moon/Mars exploration (testing instruments, rehearsing operations, finding resources or habitability, reducing risk).

The output format is open: written report, interactive map, visualization, app, story, similarity ranking, ML tool, or something else.

## Points the brief stresses

- **Don't just rediscover well-known analogs** (Atacama, Antarctica, Iceland, Hawaii). Look for places that have received less attention. "Surprise us."
- **Think beyond visual similarity.** Two places can look alike but differ in geology, environment or processes; use data to justify the analogy.
- **An analog doesn't have to match everything** — e.g. good geologically, poor thermally. Say what is similar, how similar, and what differs.
- **Processes as well as places**: sedimentation, dust storms, volcanism, erosion, ice dynamics, water-driven alteration.
- **Scale matters**: a single crater, valley or volcanic field can be a stronger match than a whole desert.
- **Whole Earth, including subsurface**: polar regions, deserts, mountains, volcanoes, impact craters, salt flats, glaciers, caves, lava tubes, mines.
- **Use multiple datasets** together.
- **Remember Earth's limits**: reduced gravity, lunar vacuum, thin Martian atmosphere and radiation can't be reproduced.

## Dimensions suggested by the brief

Geology/mineralogy/surface materials · terrain, elevation, slope, roughness, craters · temperature, illumination, environmental extremes · water, ice, permafrost, resources · dust, wind, erosion · biology, extremophiles, habitability · caves, lava tubes, subsurface · conditions for landing, mobility, navigation, robotics, human operations, construction.

## Resources listed by the organizers

- **Earth:** NASA Earthdata Search, NASA Worldview
- **Moon/Mars:** NASA Planetary Data System (PDS), LRO data (LROC, LOLA, Diviner, LEND, Mini-RF), LRO Diviner polar and resource products
- **Partners:** CSA RADARSAT Constellation Mission and RADARSAT-1 data, CSA SAR Python tutorial, ESA Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem, ESA Open Science Catalog, JAXA G-Portal
